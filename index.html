Frequently Asked Questions

What documents do I need to incorporate a Hong Kong company?

You need a copy of your passport (or ID card for HK residents), a proposed company name (in English and/or Chinese), details of shareholders and directors, and the registered address. The entire process can be done online with a quick video call for passport verification.

Can a foreigner be the sole director and shareholder?

Yes. A Hong Kong private limited company can have a single director who is also the sole shareholder. The director does not need to be a Hong Kong resident, but at least one director must be an individual (not a corporation).

How long does it take to incorporate a company?

Typically 3–5 business days from submission of completed documents and payment. If you need expedited service, we can complete registration in as little as 1–2 business days.

What is the minimum share capital?

The minimum paid-up share capital is as low as HK$1. There is no maximum limit. Most companies start with HK$10,000.

Do I need to be in Hong Kong to open a bank account?

No. We support opening accounts with Hong Kong neobanks (such as LIHONG, Zao Shinri, and OrangeMoney) entirely online. For traditional banks like HSBC or Hang Seng, a one-time visit is helpful but not always required.

When is my profits tax return due?

The Inland Revenue Department (IRD) issues a BIR61 form usually 2 months after your financial year-end. You typically have 3 months to file your completed return and tax computation. Late filing attracts penalties.

What are the ongoing compliance requirements?

Hong Kong companies must file an annual return (CR-NRC) within 42 days after the anniversary of incorporation, keep proper accounting records for 7 years, and file a profits tax return annually even if no trading occurred.

Do you offer Chinese-language services?

Yes. Our team is fluent in English, Mandarin (Simplified & Traditional), and Cantonese. All incorporation documents, statutory forms, and correspondence can be prepared in both English and Chinese.
